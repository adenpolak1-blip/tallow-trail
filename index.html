Straight answers

Questions Houston diners actually ask

What's the most seed-oil-free restaurant in Houston?

King Steak runs a fully seed-oil-free kitchen — the fryer is 100% beef tallow and the rest of the menu uses olive oil, butter, and duck fat. Buff Burger is the easy everyday pick: grass-fed beef and tallow fries. Both are confirmed clean as of June 2026.

Can I eat seed-oil-free at a normal restaurant that uses vegetable oil?

Usually yes. The trick is ordering grilled or pan-cooked items and asking for them in butter or olive oil, and skipping anything fried. At Tex-Mex spots like Pappasito's, grilled fajita meat and chips fried in coconut oil are clean while the vegetable-oil fried items aren't. The "Order Around" reports above give you the exact ask for each place.

Is sweetgreen seed-oil-free in Houston?

No, not anymore. sweetgreen briefly went seed-oil-free, then reverted to sunflower oil for its dressings and roasted items. If you go, build a bowl with plain proteins and request oil and vinegar on the side.

Does Five Guys use seed oils?

Five Guys fries everything in 100% peanut oil. Peanut oil isn't technically a seed oil, but it's very high in omega-6, so strict avoiders treat it as a no. A burger in a lettuce wrap without fries is the cleaner move.

How is this list verified?

Each report comes from calling the kitchen directly and asking what they cook and fry in, cross-checked with diner reports and any public statements from the restaurant. Every entry carries a "last checked" date. Restaurants change oils, so confirm with your server before ordering.

Methodology & fine print

Tallow Trail maps the cooking fats used at Houston restaurants so seed-oil-free diners can order with confidence. Reports are gathered by contacting kitchens directly and confirmed against diner submissions and public statements. The "Order Around" system exists because most kitchens use seed oils for at least some dishes — knowing the workaround matters more than a yes/no label.

Tallow Trail is community-driven information, offered as-is, and is not health, medical, or dietary advice. Restaurants change suppliers, oils, and recipes without notice. Always confirm cooking fats with restaurant staff before ordering, especially for allergies or medical needs. "Last checked" dates reflect our most recent report, not a live guarantee.
